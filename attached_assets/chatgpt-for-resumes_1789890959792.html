Five things people ask before they start

We're not here to tell you ChatGPT is bad. It's the most useful writing tool most of us have ever had. It's just pointed at the wrong half of the problem.

"Isn't this just ChatGPT with extra steps?"

The steps are the point. ChatGPT gets your resume. StoryCV gets your answers. Feed a model better raw material and the output changes before you touch a single adjective.

"I'm a decent writer, I can prompt my way there."

Probably. But the hard part was never phrasing, it was selection. Deciding which three things out of four years belong on the page is judgment work, and no prompt hands it back to you.

"Will a recruiter clock it as AI?"

About 62% of hiring managers say they may reject a resume they suspect is AI-written, and fully AI-generated resumes have shown roughly 23% fewer callbacks. What trips the alarm is genericness. Details from your own work read as human because they are.

"I don't have time for a conversation."

A ChatGPT resume workflow has been clocked at around 69 minutes once you count prompt tinkering and formatting. A guided interview takes only about 12 minutes. The conversation is the shortcut, not the detour.

"What about ATS?"

Handled, and honestly it's bare minimum now. Getting parsed is a formality. Being remembered by the human on the other side is the part worth fighting for.

Yes, and it's genuinely useful for grammar, tightening long bullets and varying repetitive verbs. The limit is that ChatGPT for resumes only sees the resume you paste in, so it can rearrange your existing words but it can't add the context that was never written down. That missing context is usually the part that makes a bullet worth reading.

People search for a ChatGPT resume builder, but ChatGPT is a writing tool, not a builder. There's no structure, no formatting, no ATS handling and no guidance on what to emphasise. You bring the strategy and it supplies the sentences. One comparison clocked a ChatGPT-only workflow at around 69 minutes versus roughly 12 with a purpose-built tool, most of it lost to prompt tinkering and manual formatting.

ChatGPT for CV writing hits the same wall as resume writing. It can restructure and reword what you give it, but it doesn't know which project mattered, which trade-off you made, or what would have broken without you. ChatGPT CV writing works best as a final polish pass, after you've already worked out what belongs on the page.

There isn't a prompt that fixes this. Something like “rewrite my experience in five bullet points highlighting leadership and revenue growth” only works if you already know leadership and revenue growth are the right things to highlight. If a tool needs you to know what to emphasise before it can help, the real work is still sitting on your desk. Better ChatGPT resume writing starts with better recall, not better vocabulary.

Around 62% of hiring managers say they may reject a resume they suspect is AI-generated, and pure AI-generated resumes have been found to produce roughly 23% fewer callbacks than human-written ones. What gets flagged is genericness, not AI itself. Specific details from your own work read as human because they are.

ChatGPT starts with a blank prompt box and waits for you to know what to say. StoryCV starts by asking. It interviews you about one role at a time, pulls out the decisions and outcomes you'd have skipped, then writes the bullets from that material. Everything you say is kept as your Career Story, so the next resume, LinkedIn rewrite or interview answer starts from something instead of nothing.
